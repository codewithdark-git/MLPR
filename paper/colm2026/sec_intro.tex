\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fine-tuning is the standard route for injecting new knowledge into pretrained
language models, whether through supervised instruction tuning, domain
adaptation, or parameter-efficient updates \citep{hu2022lora}. A growing body
of evidence shows that this process memorizes far more readily than it
generalizes: models fit training-format facts quickly
\citep{tirumala2022memorization,carlini2023quantifying}, while behavioral
control of the acquired knowledge---answering paraphrases, chaining facts,
or refusing when unsure---arrives late or not at all
\citep{kandpal2023large,allen2023physics}. For practitioners this gap is
expensive: a model that \emph{knows} an entity (its answer is recoverable
from its internal states) but cannot \emph{say} it under mild distribution
shift will silently fail downstream, and standard observables such as
training loss or held-out perplexity cannot distinguish the two regimes.

We propose to make this distinction measurable \emph{during} training, and
to act on it. Our approach, MLPR (Mid-Layer Probe Regularization), wraps a
parameter-efficient fine-tuning run with three coupled instruments. First,
a small linear probe $p_\phi$ attached to a mid layer $l^\star$ reads
entity identities from the backbone's hidden states; because the probe
trains on detached states during a warm-up phase, it is a pure observer
that cannot steer the backbone. Second, a teacher-forced likelihood metric
estimates recall saturation $\amemlf$ each epoch by scoring the answer
tokens given the training-format question---a ``knowing'' signal that, unlike
free-generation exact match, does not conflate knowledge with answer
extraction. Third, a saturation gate compares $\amemlf$ against a threshold
$\tau_0$ and, once crossed, ramps in a probe-alignment loss with weight
$\lambda(t)$ so that the backbone is regularized toward keeping acquired
identities linearly decodable at the mid layer. An eval-aware adaptive
controller completes the loop by damping the learning rate when held-out
loss degrades, subject to an explicit floor that keeps memorization
dynamics alive.

We instantiate MLPR on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct
\citep{qwen2024qwen25}, training each on a synthetic entity-question corpus
with a 1{,}600-entity vocabulary and full per-epoch telemetry (adapters,
probe states, gate and controller histories) for up to 50 epochs. The
resulting two-scale dataset of training dynamics yields findings we
summarize here and develop in Section~\ref{sec:results}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Recall saturation is scale-dependent}
  (Section~\ref{sec:res:sat}). Under an identical corpus and budget, the
  7B model's likelihood recall $\amemlf$ follows an S-curve that crosses
  $\tau_0{=}0.8$ at epoch 24 and reaches $0.875$ by epoch 27, whereas the
  0.5B model climbs slowly to $0.39$ over 50 epochs. The MLPR gate
  consequently opens only at the larger scale, and we observe the
  full activation transient---$\lambda(t)$ ramping from $0.015$ to $0.225$
  across three epochs---live in training logs.
  \item \textbf{Knowing and saying dissociate sharply}
  (Section~\ref{sec:res:gap}). At gate activation the 7B model recalls
  $82.5\%$ of training entities under teacher forcing, yet produces the
  exact string under free generation only $2\%$ of the time and
  transfers to paraphrased prompts $0\%$ of the time. Token-level recall
  reaches $98.7\%$, locating the failure in answer assembly rather than
  identity storage.
  \item \textbf{Probes detect memorization early and cheaply}
  (Section~\ref{sec:res:probe}). The mid-layer probe separates memorized
  from held-out entities ($7$--$11\%$ train-batch accuracy vs.\ $0\%$
  at chance $1/1600$) within a few epochs, and its loss falls orders of
  magnitude below chance at 7B, providing an early-warning signal of
  saturation that precedes any behavioral metric.
  \item \textbf{Eval-aware control needs a floor}
  (Section~\ref{sec:res:control}). Rising held-out loss is the signature
  of memorization that the gate waits for; an earlier controller design
  responded by decaying the learning rate without bound (cumulative
  multiplier $1.0{\to}0.17$), freezing exactly the dynamics it was meant
  to study. A bounded damping floor ($0.5\times$) fixes the spiral and is
  validated across two 50-epoch-class runs and unit tests.
  \item \textbf{Updates concentrate in upper-layer MLPs}
  (Section~\ref{sec:res:anatomy}). Computing
  $\Delta W = \tfrac{\alpha}{r}BA$ per LoRA-adapted projection shows
  $70$--$73\%$ of update mass in MLP projections and $56$--$58\%$ in the
  upper half of layers, consistent with factual storage accounts
  \citep{meng2022rome,geva2021ffn}.
\end{itemize}

Our contributions are: (i) a training-time instrumentation stack---probe,
likelihood-based recall metric, saturation gate, and bounded adaptive
controller---that turns the knowing--using gap into an observable,
thresholdable quantity; (ii) a two-scale study showing that recall
saturation, and hence gate activation, is scale-dependent within a fixed
compute budget, with the activation transient captured live; (iii) a
dissociation analysis separating teacher-forced recall, free-generation
exact match, substring containment, and paraphrase transfer; and (iv) a
documented failure mode and repair for eval-aware learning-rate
controllers in memorization regimes. All telemetry, code, and trained
adapters are released.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Probing internal representations.} Linear probes on hidden states
are a standard instrument for asking what models encode
\citep{alain2017probes,belinkov2022probing}; control tasks guard against
probes that exploit surface statistics \citep{hewitt2019control}. Our
probe differs in role: rather than auditing a static checkpoint, it is
trained \emph{alongside} the run (on detached states) and its readings
drive a control decision---whether to activate a regularization
pathway---turning the probe from a diagnostic into a controller input.
The probe is deliberately linear; richer readouts such as sparse
autoencoders \citep{bricken2023monosemanticity} or circuit analyses
\citep{elhage2021mathematical} could substitute in future work.

\textbf{Knowledge storage and localization.} Mechanistic and editing
studies localize factual knowledge in mid-to-upper layers, particularly
MLP blocks that behave as key--value memories
\citep{meng2022rome,meng2023memit,geva2021ffn,dai2022knowledgeneurons,
decao2021editing,mitchell2022mend}. Our anatomy analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:res:anatomy}) is consistent with this picture from the
direction of training dynamics rather than causal editing. Synthetic
entity corpora with known ground truth have been used to study capacity
and retrieval \citep{allen2023physics}; our generator follows this
tradition but exposes per-epoch recall dynamics rather than post-hoc
capacity estimates.

\textbf{Memorization dynamics.} Memorization of training data is
well-documented at scale \citep{carlini2023quantifying,zhang2017understanding},
and large models memorize \emph{more} before they overfit in loss terms
\citep{tirumala2022memorization}. Knowledge acquisition is strongly
scale-dependent \citep{kaplan2020scaling}, and long-tail facts are
learned poorly without upweighting \citep{kandpal2023large}; capacity
analyses of synthetic stores report sharp size thresholds for retrieval
\citep{allen2023physics}. Our two-scale experiment gives a fine-grained,
per-epoch view of this phenomenon in the fine-tuning regime. The
dissociation we measure between teacher-forced recall and free-generation
production extends paraphrase-consistency findings
\citep{elazar2021measuring,mallen2023pararel} into training dynamics, and
recalls the competence--expression decoupling of grokking
\citep{power2022grokking}, here driven by data format rather than weight
decay.

\textbf{Adaptive training control.} Adaptive learning-rate schedules
respond to loss plateaus or gradient statistics; our controller adds a
held-out-loss criterion with a principled twist specific to the
memorization regime: because rising held-out loss is the very signal the
gate waits for, damping must be bounded lest the controller extinguish
the phenomenon it monitors. We are not aware of prior work that analyzes
this controller--phenomenon interaction, which we document with a
negative result (an unbounded decay spiral) and its repair.
