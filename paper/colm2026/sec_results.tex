\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Corpus.} We use a synthetic entity-question corpus in the spirit
of controlled knowledge-store studies \citep{allen2023physics}: a
vocabulary of $1{,}600$ invented entities with deterministic,
single-token-friendly answers. The memorization split
$\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{mem}}$ contains 1{,}200 question--answer pairs in
a fixed training template with an \texttt{Answer:} suffix (stripped for
all evaluations); the generalization split $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{gen}}$
(450 prompts) re-asks the trained facts through \emph{chaining} and
\emph{intersection} templates never seen in training, so exact match on
it measures \emph{use} rather than recall. All entities are synthetic
strings, so pre-training leakage is excluded by construction
(Appendix~\ref{app:dataset}).

\textbf{Models and training.} We train Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and
Qwen3-1.7B \citep{qwen2024qwen25,qwen3} with LoRA ($r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$) on all seven attention and MLP
projections ($8.8$M / $17.4$M trainable parameters), probe layers
$l^\star{=}12/24$ and $14/28$, probe head $d\!\to\!1600$, backbone LR
$2{\times}10^{-4}$, probe LR $2{\times}10^{-3}$ (cosine, $5\%$ warmup),
effective batch 16 (75 steps/epoch), 24 epochs, seed 42, bf16, one
A100-40GB GPU per run on Modal. The probe optimizer group is
gradient-isolated from the backbone clip. Every run emits per-step
losses, per-epoch gate evaluations, final per-sample evaluation
records, and adapter/probe checkpoints to a persistent volume and to
Weights \& Biases.

\textbf{Arms.} At 0.5B we run the two-condition comparison: pure SFT
($\lambda\equiv 0$) and probe-gated MLPR. At 1.7B we complete the
engagement-schedule grid (the arm that was budget-censored in our
earlier study): SFT; \emph{output-gated} MLPR (gate signal
$\rho=\amemlf$, $\tau_0{=}0.8$, $\Delta_0{=}0.05$; two replicates);
\emph{probe-gated} MLPR (signal $\rho_{\mathrm{rep}}$,
$\tau_0{=}0.35$, $\Delta_0{=}0.15$; two replicates plus one 16-epoch
censored replicate); and a \emph{$\lambda$-decay} arm whose probe
weight decays linearly to zero over $D{=}8$ epochs after onset. Two
further 16-epoch censored runs replicate the early-time trajectories
(Appendix~\ref{app:curves}).

\textbf{Metrics.} Each epoch we log teacher-forced recall $\amemlf$
\eqref{eq:lf}, token-level recall, free-generation EM $\amemem$,
chaining/intersection EM $\agen$, and the probe's entity-decodability
$\rho_{\mathrm{rep}}$. Each run ends with a final $n{=}256$
per-channel evaluation whose prompt-aligned per-sample records feed a
paired McNemar test \citep{mcnemar1947} between arms
(Table~\ref{tab:main} lists the endpoints of the seven completed
24-epoch runs).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{When the gate can open is decided by the gate signal}
\label{sec:res:sat}

Figure~\ref{fig:sat} contrasts the two gate signals.
\textbf{(a)} At 0.5B the probe-saturation gate opens at epoch 3: the
probe already decodes $40.6\%$ of memorized entities from the mid
layer (crossing $\tau_0{=}0.35$) while output recall is still $0.016$;
$\lambda$ reaches $\lambda_0{=}0.3$ at epoch 4 and holds for 21
epochs. The same arm's output signal does eventually cross
$\tau_0{=}0.8$---but only at epoch 19, sixteen epochs later, and in
our earlier 50-epoch runs at $4\times$ lower backbone LR the 0.5B
output signal never approached the threshold at all (max $0.39$).
\textbf{(b)} At 1.7B the output signal \emph{is} reachable, but its
crossing time is unstable: one replicate first exceeds $\tau_0$ at
epoch 17 ($\amemlf{=}0.885$); a replicate of the identical
configuration never exceeds $0.198$. The crossing replicate also
demonstrates the gate's live re-evaluation: at epoch 18 the signal
dips back into the $[\tau_0,\tau_0{+}\Delta_0)$ band and $\lambda$
drops $0.3\to0.075$ before re-ramping---the mechanism tracks the
signal, not a schedule.

\subsection{The engagement schedule decides whether memorization completes}
\label{sec:res:sched}

Figure~\ref{fig:sched} shows the payoff of the 1.7B grid: five arms,
identical data, seed, and optimizer, differing only in how the probe
loss engages. Three regimes emerge.
\emph{(i) Late, output-triggered engagement reaches SFT parity.} The
arm that opened at epoch 17 finished at $\amemlf{=}0.926$ vs.\ the
SFT control's $0.934$ (McNemar $b{=}12$, $c{=}10$, $p{=}0.83$), and
exercised the regularized phase for 8 full epochs at
$\lambda_0{=}0.3$: recall rose from $0.698$ at activation to $0.926$
at the final evaluation with no instability. The phase our earlier
large-scale run truncated after 3 epochs is here \emph{completed},
stable, and harmless to recall.
\emph{(ii) Early sustained probe injection is high-variance.} The two
sustained replicates opened the gate one epoch apart (5 vs.\ 6) yet
finished $0.805$ and $0.109$, both below SFT (rep.\,1: $b{=}40$,
$c{=}7$, $p{=}1.1{\times}10^{-6}$; rep.\,2: $b{=}211$, $c{=}0$,
$p{=}6{\times}10^{-64}$): early probe gradients do not accelerate
output-level memorization at 1.7B.
\emph{(iii) Transient engagement is the worst outcome.} The
$\lambda$-decay arm engaged for 8 epochs, then lost the probe loss
entirely; its recall finished at $0.012$---an order of magnitude
below every other arm---while its representation decodability reached
$0.948$ (Section~\ref{sec:res:gap}).
Finally, the never-engaged replicate doubles as an SFT replication
(its probe is detached, $\lambda\equiv0$): its takeoff lagged the
control by about seven epochs and it was still mid-transition
($0.227$, rising) at epoch 24. Memorization onset at this scale is
therefore \emph{stochastic across runs}---precisely why engagement
should be gated on a monitored signal, and why the signal must be one
that actually fires.

\subsection{Knowing is not saying, at either scale}
\label{sec:res:gap}

Figure~\ref{fig:gap}a reports the endpoint channels for all arms.
Teacher-forced recall reaches $0.91$--$0.93$ at both scales (and
$0.81$ for the sustained replicate), token-level recall $0.99$, yet
free-generation EM is $\le 0.012$ in \emph{every} arm and
generalization EM $\le 0.004$. The saying gap is invariant across a
$3.4\times$ parameter range and across all engagement schedules;
regularizing the mid layer does not, by itself, close it.
Figure~\ref{fig:gap}b plots the sharpest dissociation in the grid,
the $\lambda$-decay arm: its probe decodability climbs to $0.948$
(the representation organizes the entities essentially perfectly)
while output recall never leaves $0.012$. Knowing---in the strong,
linearly decodable sense---is demonstrably present without saying;
the two capabilities are separable training targets, not two ends of
one curve.

\subsection{Dynamics and the probe as early-warning monitor}
\label{sec:res:control}

Figure~\ref{fig:dyn}a shows the shared dynamics. CE plateaus at
$\approx$1.4 (0.5B) and $\approx$2.7 (1.7B) once the template is
learned, then drops late (epochs 12--16 at 1.7B) to $0.04$--$0.09$ in
the arms whose recall completes; the three arms still mid-transition
at epoch 24 ($\lambda$-decay, sustained rep.\,2, never-engaged)
stall at $0.51$--$0.64$ instead, and in the decay arm the stall sets
in right after the probe weight is removed. Until their gates open,
the MLPR arms' CE is indistinguishable from their SFT controls: the
detached probe is a pure observer during warm-up. The probe loss
(Fig.~\ref{fig:dyn}b) falls from its random-head value ($\sim$17--18)
to $0.05$--$0.16$, two orders of magnitude below chance
$\ln 1600\approx 7.38$, within 20 epochs at both scales.

Figure~\ref{fig:dyn}c overlays $\rho_{\mathrm{rep}}(t)$ for all six
MLPR arms. The representation saturates early everywhere: the probe
crosses $0.5$ between epochs 4 and 7 at both scales, while output
recall crosses $0.5$ only at epochs 15--17 in the arms that get there
at all, and never in three arms (Fig.~\ref{fig:dyn}d). The probe's
head start is 13 epochs at 0.5B and 8--18 epochs at 1.7B---the
measurable form of ``knowing precedes saying'': the mid-layer
representation organizes acquired facts long before the LM head
reliably produces them.

\subsection{Update anatomy: upper-layer MLPs carry the memorization}
\label{sec:res:anatomy}

From the released final adapters we compute
$\Delta W = \tfrac{\alpha}{r} BA$ per adapted projection
(Fig.~\ref{fig:anat}). MLP projections carry $71.1\%$ (0.5B) and
$65.0\%$ (1.7B) of total update mass $\sum_l \lVert \Delta W
\rVert_F$; key/value attention projections are nearly untouched; and
the top three (layer, module) cells are gate/up/down-projection in
every case---at 1.7B they are the top-layer gate projections (layers
21--23 of 28) of the post-activation arm, so the concentration holds
\emph{after} eight epochs of probe-gradient flow, not merely in an
un-activated regime. At 0.5B the mass is more mid-layer ($46.1\%$ in
the upper half vs.\ $55.1\%$ at 1.7B).
