\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{The knowing--using gap as a first-class object.} Our results
argue for treating the gap between internal recall and behavioral
production as a measurable training-time quantity rather than a
post-hoc evaluation surprise. The likelihood metric and the mid-layer
probe are cheap (a forward pass per epoch; a linear head), and together
they separate regimes that aggregate observables conflate:
not-yet-known, known-but-unsayable, and---in the $\lambda$-decay
arm---representation-known-but-output-never-formed.

\textbf{The gate signal and its schedule are design axes.} The same
formal gate behaves very differently depending on what it watches and
for how long. An output-likelihood gate defers engagement until the
model can recall---principled, but its onset is stochastic (one
replicate crossed at epoch 17; another never crossed), and a schedule
that silently never fires is indistinguishable from plain SFT. The
probe-saturation gate fires early at both scales because a fresh
linear head measures representation organization rather than LM-head
surface learning. What decided the outcome was not whether probe
gradients flowed, but \emph{when and for how long}: late,
output-triggered engagement at full weight matched SFT recall; early
sustained injection was high-variance and below SFT; and transient
injection that decayed to zero left the model at $0.012$ recall while
its mid layer organized the entities at $94.8\%$ decodability---even
though the arm then received twelve epochs of pure SFT and never
recovered. Probe regularization should therefore engage \emph{after}
saturation and then \emph{persist}; both halves of that prescription
are testable with the released harness.

\textbf{The saying gap is invariant to what we varied.} No arm at
either scale exceeded $1.2\%$ free-generation EM or $0.4\%$
generalization EM, while token-level recall under forcing reached
$99\%$: the gap lives at the representation-to-surface interface, and
closing it plausibly requires assembly-oriented objectives or decoding
interventions rather than better-organized storage.

\textbf{Controllers must not fight the phenomenon.} The decay-spiral
case study (Appendix~\ref{app:spiral}) is a small but concrete
instance of a general design error: an adaptive controller aimed at a
target regime treated that regime's signature as a fault and drove the
system away from it. The floor repair converts the controller from
opposed to subordinate: damp instability, never extinguish the
dynamics under study.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our findings are bounded in four ways. First, the corpus is synthetic
and single-domain: crossing epochs and gap magnitudes will differ on
natural knowledge (e.g., long-tail Wikipedia facts
\citep{kandpal2023large}). Second, each schedule is represented by one
or two replicates, run-to-run variance at 1.7B is itself large
(sustained arms: $0.109$ vs.\ $0.805$), and GPU non-determinism makes
even same-seed runs diverge in takeoff epoch; our schedule claims rest
on paired tests against a common SFT control, but single-run
comparisons at this scale are unreliable. Third, the 24-epoch budget
censors late-takeoff arms: the never-engaged replicate was still
rising when it ended ($0.227$), so its endpoint is a lower bound.
Fourth, the probe is linear and trained on the corpus it monitors,
all experiments use LoRA at a single rank, and the mechanism behind
the decay-arm collapse---like that of the sustained arms'
variance---is hypothesized rather than established; the released
per-step telemetry is intended to make such follow-ups cheap.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented MLPR, a training-time instrumentation-and-control layer
that makes the knowing--using gap measurable online and acts on it
through a saturation gate. In a two-scale engagement-schedule grid we
showed that the gate signal decides when engagement is possible
(probe decodability fires at epochs 3--6, the output signal 11--16
epochs later or never), that the engagement schedule decides whether
memorization completes at all (final recall $0.012$--$0.934$ under
identical data, seed, and optimizer, with late output-triggered
engagement reaching SFT parity by McNemar test), captured the gate's
live re-evaluation transient, measured a knowing/saying dissociation
that no schedule closes (generalization EM $\le 0.4\%$ everywhere)
and a representation-without-output regime induced by transient
engagement, and confirmed from released adapters that memorization
updates concentrate in upper-layer MLP projections. Transferring the
instruments to natural corpora, scaling replicates beyond two per
arm, and pairing the regularized phase with assembly-oriented
objectives are immediate next steps.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work studies memorization dynamics, which sit at the intersection
of capability and privacy: the same teacher-forced recall metric that
monitors learning could, in adversarial hands, audit models for
memorized personal data, and the recall/production dissociation we
document cautions that exact-match audits may \emph{underestimate}
what a model has stored. We release only synthetic entities invented
for this study, and we believe better monitoring instruments
ultimately serve privacy auditing and data governance more than they
enable extraction.

\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
All code (dataset generator, trainer, callbacks, gate and engagement
schedules, controller, Modal deployment, analysis, McNemar module, and
figure scripts) is included in the released codebase, together with
the exact configurations of Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}. For every
production run we release the final adapter and probe head, the
per-epoch gate history, the complete stdout log, and a per-sample
final evaluation record whose prompt-aligned correctness vectors are
the direct inputs of every McNemar test reported here; per-epoch
numbers in every figure and in Appendix~\ref{app:curves} are
traceable to these artifacts, identified by their Weights \& Biases
IDs in Appendix~\ref{app:artifacts}. The earlier 50-epoch-class runs
behind the decay-spiral case study and the scale-saturation pilots
are the v4-generation artifacts also listed there.
