\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{The knowing--using gap as a first-class object.} Our results
argue for treating the gap between internal recall and behavioral
production as a measurable training-time quantity rather than a
post-hoc evaluation surprise. The likelihood metric and the mid-layer
probe are cheap (a forward pass per epoch; a linear head), and together
they separate three regimes that aggregate observables conflate:
not-yet-known, known-but-unsayable, and said-but-not-transferred.
Monitoring the saturation curve converts ``when do we stop
memorizing?'' from a guess into a threshold.

\textbf{Scale determines whether gating is possible.} The 0.5B result
is as informative as the 7B one: below some capacity--data interaction
threshold, likelihood recall plateaus far under $\tau_0$ and a
saturation-gated intervention simply never activates. Gate-based
regularization thus has a minimum viable scale (or data volume) for a
given corpus, and pilots below that scale will silently do nothing
beyond SFT.

\textbf{Answer assembly is the bottleneck, not storage.} The
containment finding ($76.5\%$ substring vs.\ $2\%$ EM at 7B,
token recall $98.7\%$) localizes the production failure in surface
realization, consistent with paraphrase-consistency findings
\citep{elazar2021measuring,mallen2023pararel}; it argues for
complementing the post-activation phase with assembly-oriented
objectives such as format-canonical decoding or constrained emission.

\textbf{Controllers must not fight the phenomenon.} The decay-spiral
case study is a small but concrete instance of a general design error:
an adaptive controller aimed at a target regime treated that regime's
signature (rising held-out loss) as a fault and drove the system away
from it. The floor repair is deliberately crude---one constant---but it
converts the controller from opposed to subordinate: damp instability,
never extinguish the dynamics under study.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Our findings are bounded in four ways. First, the corpus is synthetic
and single-domain: entity-question memorization is the cleanest setting
for the recall metric, but crossing epochs and gap magnitudes will
differ on natural knowledge (e.g., long-tail Wikipedia facts
\citep{kandpal2023large}). Second, the 7B regularized phase is
truncated at three epochs of $\lambda>0$; all claims about it are
stability observations, not outcome claims. Third, the probe is linear
and trained on the corpus it monitors; control-task or cross-corpus
validation \citep{hewitt2019control} would strengthen the monitoring
claim, although the held-out-entity accuracy already provides a
built-in negative control. Fourth, all experiments use LoRA at a single
rank; the floor value $m_{\min}{=}0.5$ is a robustness prior rather than
a tuned constant, and other ranks may shift the anatomy split.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented MLPR, a training-time instrumentation-and-control layer
that makes the knowing--using gap measurable online and acts on it
through a saturation gate. In a controlled two-scale study we showed
that recall saturation---and therefore gate activation---is
scale-dependent, captured the activation transient live at 7B, measured
a sharp knowing/saying dissociation localized in answer assembly,
identified probes as early-warning monitors, documented and repaired an
unbounded damping failure in eval-aware controllers, and confirmed from
released adapters that memorization updates concentrate in upper-layer
MLP projections. Completing the regularized 7B phase and transferring
the instruments to natural knowledge corpora are immediate next steps.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
This work studies memorization dynamics, which sit at the intersection
of capability and privacy: the same teacher-forced recall metric that
monitors learning could, in adversarial hands, audit models for
memorized personal data, and the containment/exact-match dissociation
we document cautions that exact-match audits may \emph{underestimate}
what a model has stored. We release only synthetic entities invented
for this study, no personal or web-scraped content, and we believe
better monitoring instruments ultimately serve privacy auditing and
data governance more than they enable extraction.
