\begin{abstract}
When a language model is fine-tuned on new factual content, two capabilities
diverge: the model quickly becomes able to \emph{recall} an answer when the
question is posed in the training format (knowing), while its ability to
\emph{produce} the answer under distribution shift lags far behind (using).
We introduce MLPR, a lightweight monitoring-and-regularization layer that
makes this knowing--using gap measurable and actionable during training: a
linear probe at a mid layer $l^\star$ reads entity identities from hidden
states, and a saturation gate ramps in a probe-alignment loss
$\lambda(t)$ once a monitored signal crosses a threshold $\tau_0$. We
study Qwen2.5-0.5B and Qwen3-1.7B under an engagement-schedule grid of
ten fully instrumented runs on a synthetic entity-question corpus,
comparing two gate signals (output-likelihood recall vs.\
representation-space probe decodability) and three engagement schedules
(sustained, decayed, output-triggered). Three findings emerge. First,
the gate signal decides when engagement is possible: the probe signal
opens the gate at epoch 3--6 at both scales, 11--16 epochs before the
output signal crosses its own threshold in the replicates where it
ever does, and in one output-gated replicate it never crosses within
budget. Second, the engagement
schedule decides whether memorization completes: late output-triggered
engagement reaches SFT recall parity ($0.926$ vs.\ $0.934$; paired
McNemar $p{=}0.83$) and completes a previously budget-censored
regularized phase with 8 stable epochs at full probe weight, whereas
early sustained injection is high-variance ($0.109$--$0.805$) and
transient injection with decay to zero collapses recall to $0.012$
while the representation still decodes $94.8\%$ of entities---the
sharpest knowing--saying dissociation we observe. Third, free-
generation and generalization exact match stay at $\le 0.4\%$ in
\emph{every} arm at both scales even as teacher-forced recall reaches
$0.93$: the saying gap is scale-invariant, and all parameter updates
concentrate in upper-layer MLP projections. Code, telemetry, and
trained adapters are released.
\end{abstract}
