\begin{abstract}
When a language model is fine-tuned on new factual content, two capabilities
diverge: the model quickly becomes able to \emph{recall} an answer when the
question is posed in the training format (knowing), while its ability to
\emph{produce} the answer under distribution shift lags far behind (using).
We introduce MLPR, a lightweight monitoring-and-regularization layer that
makes this knowing--using gap measurable and actionable during training: a
linear probe at a mid layer $l^\star$ reads entity identities from hidden
states, a teacher-forced likelihood metric estimates recall saturation
$\amemlf$ online, and a saturation gate ramps in a probe-alignment loss
$\lambda(t)$ only once recall crosses a threshold $\tau_0$. We study
Qwen2.5-0.5B and 7B-Instruct models trained for up to 50 epochs on a
synthetic entity-question corpus with full telemetry. Three findings emerge.
First, recall saturation is strongly scale-dependent: the 7B model crosses
$\tau_0{=}0.8$ at epoch 24 ($\amemlf{=}0.825$ by epoch 25, $0.875$ by
epoch 27), while the 0.5B model plateaus at $0.39$ after 50 epochs---the
gate opens only at scale. Second, knowing and saying dissociate sharply:
at the moment of gate activation the 7B model recalls $82.5\%$ of entities
under teacher forcing yet answers only $2\%$ by free-generation exact match
and $0\%$ on paraphrased generalization prompts. Third, a mid-layer probe
detects memorization long before behavioral saturation, and all parameter
updates concentrate in upper-layer MLP projections. We further identify and
repair a failure mode of eval-aware learning-rate controllers---an
unbounded decay spiral---with a bounded damping floor that preserves
memorization dynamics. Code, telemetry, and trained adapters are released.
\end{abstract}
